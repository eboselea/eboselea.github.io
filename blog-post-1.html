My Reflection on Open Source Software through 10 weeks

It's been about 10 weeks since I started my journey into Open Source Software Engineering, and I have learned a lot about the open source process, the importance of collaboration, and the impact that open source software has on the tech industry. In this reflection, I will discuss my experiences, challenges, and key takeaways from this course. As I looked back on the past 10 weeks, I realized that working in open source software, your contributions can have a significant impact on the community. It's one of the coolest things about this field - seeing your work being used and contributing to a project that's much bigger than yourself As a student, the idea of having your work used and critiqued by others besides your classmates and professors is both exciting and intimitdating. Exiting because your work is being used and appreciated by other, and intimidating because your work has a lot more eyes on it, meaning you have to be more careful and thorough with your work to avoid creating bigger problems for the project. As a contributer, you have to be willing to accept feedback and criticism and use it to improve your work. You also have be respectful and considerate of other contributors, as open source projects are often collaborative efforts that require effective communication and teamwork. This comes with its own challenges, as you may have to navigat different opinions, work styles, cultures, personalities, and even time zones, which can affect collaboration and communication. But, the challenges are often worth it because you end up creating work that is robust, well-tested, and more likely to be adopted by the community. I believe that open source software is an effective method for building critical software, even despite the flaws and obstacles that come with it. One major aspect of open source software is ethics. This type of work requires honesty, integrity, and a commitment to making the software better for everyone from when it was first created. Not copying or stealing code, giving credit where credit is due, and being transparent about your work are all important ethical considerations in an open source community. On the technical side, open source software often requires a good understanding of software development principles, such as version control, testing, documentation, and code review. Having multiple people review your work can help catch bugs, improve code quality, and ensure the software meets the needs of the community. At the same times, when i'm looking at the work of other contributers, I have to be careful to provide constructive feedback that is respectful and helpful, rather than critical or dismissive. I can also help catch issues or bugs that the original author may have missed, and contribute to the overall quality of the software. Overall, these skills and practices are essential for building high-quality software while also fostering a positive and collaborative community.
