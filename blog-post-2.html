Post Mid-term reflection

We're now more than halfway through the semester, and I feel like I've learned a lot about open source software development and the open source community. In this reflection, I will discuss my experiences, challenges, and key takeaways from the course as we get closer to the end of the semester. In terms of what went well, I feel like I have a good understanding of the open source process and the importance of collaboration. I see why it's so important to give good feedback when reviewing other contributers's work, sometimes reviewing the work multiple times to make sure that the work is good and that it meets the standards of the project. I also feel like I have a better understanding of the open source community and the impact that open source software has on the tech industry. I feel like I have a better understanding of how to contribute to open source projects, and I feel more confident in my ability to do so. I also had a good experience with my first pull request, which was merged successfully. It was a great feeling to see my work being used and appreciated by the community. I also feel like I have a better understanding of the importance of documentation and testing in open source projects, and I feel more confident in my ability to contribute to these aspects of a project. In terms of what didn't go well, I feel like I could have been more proactive in seeking out issues to work on. I also feel like I could have been more proactive in seeking out feedback on my work, and I feel like I could have been more proactive in seeking out opportunities to contribute to the community. I still don't feel like I dedicate enough time out of class to working on issues and reviewing pull requests. I've actually done more reviewing of other contributers' work than I have done in terms of my own contributions, and I feel like I need to work on that. So one thing i'm going to do as my action plan is to have a set amount of time each week outside of class to work on issues. This will be done via Google calendar, where I will set aside at least an hour every day to mostly work on issues, and if possible, review other contributers' work. THis not only helps my time management, but also helps me become mor eproactive because I have a clear plan on when I want to work, the time I spend working, and what I want to work on.

Progress Update

In terms of my progress, I feel like I have made strides in terms of my technical skills and understanding of open source software, but it hasn't really translated to many story points or contributions to my communities. So far I only have 1 merged pull request, but now that I have cas up and running, I have taken on some more issues and am in the process of fixing them. I have also done about 2 or 3 reviews of other contributers' work in the meantime, and they've been helpful in terms of learning more about the open source process and how to contribute to open source projects. Overall, I feel like I have a decent understanding of the open source process, and now it's just a matter of gradually getting more work done and continuing to learn and improve my skills. A big learning curve for me is just finding there the root of the issue is. Some of these repositories have a lot of files and a lot of code, and it can be difficult to find where the issue is coming from. I feel like I have gotten better at this, but it's still something I need to work on. The tasks I have been working on are a bit more difficult than the ones I was working on before, and they require a bit more time to complete. But I also think they are helping me improve my skills and become a better contributer to the open source community. Overall, I feel like I have made good progress in terms of my technical skills and understanding of open source software, but I still have a lot to learn and improve on.
